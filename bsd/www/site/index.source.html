01 / A machine with presence

Computing you can see,
hear, and touch.

Sixteen bits. Spinning disks. A room full of character. This PDP-11/83 is part of Dave's collection of machines built when computers came in cabinets and storage had handles.

Inside, a Mentec M11 processor brings roughly four times the speed of a stock J11. Outside, the removable disks, magnetic tape, and terminal tell the story of a different pace of computing.

The operating system is 2.11BSD UNIX. Files, processes, a shell, networking, and sendmail: familiar ideas, running on wonderfully unfamiliar hardware.

02 / When storage had presence

A disk was something you could hold.

[ 01 ]

Two RL02 drives

Removable cartridge disks

About 10 MB per cartridge, with a physical disk pack you can lift out and put on a shelf. Swapping storage was an actual hands-on operation.

[ 02 ]

RA82

Fixed-disk storage

Around 622 MB in a serious piece of machinery. Adding disk capacity meant making room in a cabinet, not slipping a card into a slot.

[ 03 ]

TS11

Nine-track magnetic tape

Reel-to-reel storage for moving and preserving data. Tape is sequential: finding a file means physically winding your way to it. Patience was part of the interface.

How does a 16-bit computer address megabytes of memory?

A CPU's word size and the size of physical memory are different things. Memory management maps a program's smaller address space into a larger physical address space. UNIX keeps different programs in different regions of RAM; a single program does not see all that memory as one flat expanse. Working within those boundaries is part of the art of PDP-11 software.
